Role names, pay and training

Use the role and compensation in the current vacancy and your own offer. We have not verified one current salary table or a single interview duration that applies across Infosys graduate hiring.

Infosys describes its Foundation Program at the Global Education Center in Mysuru. Ask your recruiter which training arrangements apply to your offer.

Technical questions to practise

  • Your project, then one level deeper. Same opening as everywhere. But A useful follow-up is “what would you change if you rebuilt it today?” Prepare a real answer; it separates candidates who built the project from those who watched it get built.
  • OOP, thoroughly. The four pillars with examples from your own project, not textbook definitions. Abstraction vs encapsulation trips more freshers than any coding question.
  • SQL and DBMS. Joins, normalisation up to 3NF in plain words, primary vs unique key. If your resume says a database, expect a query written live.
  • A short program, watched live: palindrome, Fibonacci, count vowels, reverse a string without built-ins. They're watching your thinking-out-loud, not your syntax memory.
  • Software engineering vocabulary: SDLC stages, what Agile means in one sentence, what testing you did on your project. Services interviews love process questions because delivery is process.

HR answers to practise for Infosys

  • Why this role? Choose one specific responsibility from the current posting and connect it to something you have done or want to learn.
  • When can you join, and can you relocate? Give your actual availability and constraints. Correct any old information in your application.
  • What are the offer terms? Read the current pay, training, location and any agreement before accepting. Ask questions if anything is unclear.

Mistakes to avoid during practice

  • Textbook OOP answers with no example. The follow-up “show me that in your project” is where memorised definitions die.
  • Contradicting the application form on relocation, gaps or backlogs, the classic avoidable rejection everywhere in mass hiring.
  • Treating the HR minutes casually because the technical minutes went well. Prepare both technical and HR answers.
  • Panic freezes. Ask for ten seconds, structure, answer. The pause habit is trainable: how to stop blanking out.
