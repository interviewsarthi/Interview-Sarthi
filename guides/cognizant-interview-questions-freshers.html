Technical questions to practise

  • “Walk me through your final-year project”: then one drill question per claim. Know what you built, what breaks under load, and what you'd change today.
  • One language, honestly declared. Java: OOP pillars with real examples, exception handling, collections basics. Python: lists vs tuples, dictionaries, a simple file read. C: pointers and strings. The questions follow whatever your resume claims.
  • SQL practice: a join written live, WHERE vs HAVING, primary vs foreign key. Explain why the query gives the expected result.
  • Definition ladders: what is DBMS → normalisation in plain words; what is an operating system → process vs thread. Two clean sentences beat a memorised lecture.
  • For higher tracks: live coding, arrays, strings, recursion, with your thinking narrated. Practising out loud matters more than more problems.

HR answers to practise for Cognizant GenC

  • Why this role? Choose one specific responsibility from the current posting and connect it to something you have done or want to learn.
  • When can you join, and can you relocate? Give your actual availability and constraints. Correct any old information in your application.
  • What are the offer terms? Read the current pay, training, location and any agreement before accepting. Ask questions if anything is unclear.

Three worked answers, with follow-ups

These are original practice examples, not Cognizant's actual interview questions. The project is fictional; replace it with work you can explain and use the responsibilities in your own invitation or posting.

1. Walk me through your project. What did you own?

“Our class project was a task tracker using Flask and SQLite. I built the endpoints for creating and assigning tasks. I checked for missing titles and unknown assignees, then added tests for those inputs. That let me explain both the request handling and how I verified it.”

Why this works: project → personal contribution → validation → evidence. It avoids taking credit for the whole team's work.

Follow-ups: Why Flask? What did your tests assert? What would happen if the database write failed?

2. What is the difference between WHERE and HAVING?

“WHERE filters the rows before grouping. HAVING filters the groups after aggregation. To find customers with at least three paid orders, I would select the paid rows with WHERE, group by customer, then filter the counts with HAVING.”

SELECT customer_id, COUNT(*) AS order_count
FROM orders
WHERE status = 'paid'
GROUP BY customer_id
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 3;

Follow-ups: Why cannot COUNT(*) go in this query's WHERE clause? What changes if unpaid orders should also count? Technical reference: PostgreSQL aggregate functions.

3. Why are you interested in this role?

“The posting mentions [specific responsibility]. In [your actual project], I worked on [relevant task]. I would like to develop that skill further in this role, especially [a responsibility the posting actually describes].”

Make it yours: replace every bracket with a fact from the posting or your experience. Do not memorise a claim about Cognizant that you have not checked.

Follow-ups: Which responsibility interests you most? What have you already done to prepare? Which skill would you need to learn first?

Mistakes to avoid during practice

  • Preparing for the wrong tier: GenC-level prep in a higher-track interview.
  • A resume language you can't code in when asked live.
  • Contradicting the application form on relocation, backlogs or dates.
  • Badmouthing your college, an internship, or another company.
  • Panic silence. Ask for ten seconds, structure, answer, the pause is trainable.
