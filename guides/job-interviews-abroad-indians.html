Job interviews abroad for Indians: what changes in the US, UK, Canada and UAE

Same skills, different format. What surprises Indian candidates in interviews outside India, and how to prepare for each country.

Updated 5 October 2026 · 7 min read · Published by Interview Sarthi, makers of Prep Sarthi (AI mock interviews) and Live Sarthi (interview help on Windows)

Short answer: Interviews in the US, UK, Canada and the Gulf test the same skills as in India, but the format differs. Expect fewer questions about marks and CTC and more “tell me about a time” questions answered with real examples. Salary is discussed as annual pay (monthly in the Gulf), and you will be asked directly about your right to work. Practise those three things out loud and most of the surprises disappear.

Not immigration or legal advice. Visa and work-permit rules change. Check the official source for your situation (USCIS in the US, GOV.UK in the UK, IRCC in Canada, MoHRE in the UAE) and your university or employer's immigration adviser.

The five questions you will almost certainly get

  1. “Tell me about yourself.” Keep it to 60–90 seconds and lead with work: what you do now, one result you are proud of, and why this role is the next step. Leave out family background, school percentages and your full education history unless they ask.
  2. “Tell me about a time when…” Behavioural questions are the core of most interviews abroad. Answer with a short story: the situation, your task, what you did (say “I”, not “we”) and the result, with a number if you have one. This is the STAR method, and the STAR guide has worked examples.
  3. “Will you require sponsorship?” Answer in one honest sentence and move on. Scripts for OPT, H-1B, the UK Graduate route and Canadian work permits are in the sponsorship guide.
  4. “Why this company?” or “Why this country?” Name something specific about the employer's product, customers or work, and a professional reason for the move. “Better opportunities” on its own sounds like you would take any job.
  5. “What are your salary expectations?” Give a researched range in the local currency and format (annual in the US, UK and Canada; monthly in the UAE). Never quote an Indian CTC converted at the exchange rate.

Interviewing from India for a job abroad

  • Confirm the time zone in writing. “10 a.m. Eastern” is 7:30 or 8:30 p.m. in India depending on daylight saving. Put the interview in your calendar in the employer's time zone and check it twice.
  • Test the call app the day before (Teams, Zoom, Google Meet, Webex): camera, microphone, a plain background and a wired connection if your Wi-Fi drops.
  • Say when you can start and relocate. Employers abroad plan around notice periods and visa timelines; a clear “my notice period is 60 days and I can relocate by February” helps them more than “as soon as possible”.

Indian-English phrases that confuse interviewers abroad

These are normal at home and in Indian offices, but they slow down an interviewer in New York, London, Toronto or Dubai. Swap them before the call:

Instead ofSay
“I passed out in 2024”“I graduated in 2024”
“I have a doubt”“I have a question”
“I will revert”“I will get back to you”
“Can we prepone it?”“Can we move it earlier?”
“Please do the needful”Say the actual request: “Could you send the offer details?”
“My CTC is 18 LPA”“My base salary is about … a year” (convert, and say base and bonus separately)
“I was out of station”“I was travelling”

Speak a little slower than you do at home and finish sentences clearly. Nobody expects you to change your accent; they need to follow your examples.

Guides for each country

  • United States: recruiter screens, STAR answers, salary research with public H-1B wage data.
  • United Kingdom: competency-based questions, right to work checks and the sponsor register.
  • Canada: the “Canadian experience” question, behavioural rounds and references.
  • Dubai and the UAE: salary packages, gratuity on basic pay, notice periods and fake offers.
  • Germany: the structured format, German-language questions, salary as annual gross.
  • Australia: behavioural questions, local experience, salary plus super.
  • NHS interviews for Indian nurses: values, scenario questions, NEWS2 and SBAR.
  • Amazon: the 16 Leadership Principles and how the loop probes your stories.
  • STAR answers: the method behind behavioural questions everywhere, with examples.
