What to say when a client asks something you don’t know

It happens in every client meeting: a question about a date, a number or a detail you don’t have in front of you. A calm, honest line and a clear follow-up keep the client’s trust. A guess does not.

Updated 12 November 2026 · 5 min read · Published by Interview Sarthi, makers of Meeting Sarthi (help in client meetings on Windows)

Short answer: Never guess a date, a number or a commitment. Say what you do know, say when you will confirm the rest, write the question down, and reply before the time you promised. “Let me confirm that and get back to you by end of day today.”

Why a guess costs more than “I’ll confirm”

Whatever you say in a client meeting becomes something the client remembers, and often something they repeat to their own manager. A guessed go-live date turns into a promise. Correcting it a week later costs far more trust than saying “I’ll confirm” today. Clients rarely mind a short wait for a correct answer. They do mind a wrong one.

Lines that work

You need to check an exact detail

“Let me confirm that and get back to you by end of day today.”

You know part of the answer

“What I can tell you now is that testing is in progress. The exact date I’ll confirm by tomorrow morning.”

The question is not clear

“Just so I answer the right thing: is this about the payment flow or the reports?”

It is another team’s area

“That sits with our infrastructure team. I’ll get their answer and share it with you by Thursday.”

It is about money or numbers

“I don’t want to give you a wrong figure. I’ll send the numbers in writing today.”

Each line does three things: it is honest, it gives a time, and it keeps the meeting moving. Avoid “soon” and “I’ll check internally” with no time attached.

Then follow up

  1. Write the question down in the meeting, in the client’s words.
  2. Give a time, not “soon”. End of day, tomorrow morning, Thursday.
  3. Put it in the recap you send after the meeting, under your action items.
  4. Reply before the time you promised. If you cannot, tell the client before the deadline, not after.

Prepare, so it happens less

Most client questions are predictable: status, dates, blockers, what changed since last time, and cost. Before the meeting, write down the five questions you are most likely to get and the answer to each. Keep your project status in writing, with dates and numbers, so you are not answering from memory.

Questions people ask

Is it okay to say "I don't know" to a client?

Yes, as long as you add when you will find out. "Let me confirm that and get back to you by end of day" is honest and professional. A guess that turns out wrong does far more harm.

How quickly should I follow up?

Give a specific time in the meeting and reply before it. Small details are best answered the same day. If you cannot make the time you gave, tell the client before it passes.

What if I already gave the client a wrong date?

Correct it as soon as you know, in writing, with the right date and a short reason. An early correction keeps trust; a late surprise loses it.
