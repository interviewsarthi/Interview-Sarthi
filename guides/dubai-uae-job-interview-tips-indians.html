Dubai and UAE job interviews for Indians: questions, salary package and notice period

For Indians interviewing from India or already in the UAE: what recruiters ask, and how the package really works.

Updated 5 October 2026 · 7 min read · Published by Interview Sarthi, makers of Prep Sarthi (AI mock interviews) and Live Sarthi (interview help on Windows)

Short answer: UAE interviews move fast and are often run by recruiters or agencies. Expect questions on your notice period, your visa status and your expected salary as a monthly package in AED. Know the difference between basic salary and allowances, because end-of-service gratuity is calculated on basic pay. Ask for the breakdown in writing before you accept.

Not immigration or legal advice. Visa and work-permit rules change. Check the official source for your situation (USCIS in the US, GOV.UK in the UK, IRCC in Canada, MoHRE in the UAE) and your university or employer's immigration adviser.

Questions you will be asked

  • “What is your notice period?” Often the first question. Give the exact number of days and whether it can be bought out or shortened.
  • “Where are you now, and on what visa?” In India, on a UAE visit visa, or employed in the UAE. It decides how fast you can join.
  • “What is your expected salary?” Expect it to mean a monthly figure in AED.
  • “Do you have GCC experience?” If not, name experience with Gulf clients, Middle East markets or multinational teams.
  • “Why the UAE?” Give a professional reason (the sector, the company, the market) rather than only tax-free pay.
  • “Do you hold a driving licence?” Common for sales and field roles.

The salary package, explained

Offers in the UAE are usually monthly and split into parts. A typical breakdown:

PartWhat it means for you
Basic salaryThe base figure. End-of-service gratuity is calculated on it, so a higher basic is worth more over time.
Housing allowanceOften the largest allowance; some employers provide accommodation instead.
Transport and other allowancesFixed monthly amounts added to basic.
BenefitsMedical insurance, annual flight home, visa costs and sometimes school fees; ask which are included.

The UAE has no personal income tax on salary, so compare offers on the full monthly package, not the basic alone. Never compare an Indian CTC converted at the exchange rate; compare what you would spend and save in each city.

Script

“I'm looking for a total package of around 18,000 dirhams a month. Could you share how it would split between basic and allowances, and which benefits are included?”

An example figure only: research the role on job sites and with people already working in the UAE in your field.

Probation and notice periods

Under the UAE labour law, probation can last up to six months, and contracts usually set a notice period between 30 and 90 days. Read both in your offer letter before you resign from your current job.

How to spot a fake offer

  • Never pay to get a job. UAE rules prohibit recruitment agencies from charging job seekers fees. A request for “visa processing” or “document” money is the most common scam.
  • Be wary of an offer with no real interview, a personal email address such as Gmail for a large company, or pressure to pay within a day.
  • Check the company's official website and LinkedIn page, and find the recruiter on the company's site, not only through the email you received.

Indian-English phrases that confuse interviewers abroad

These are normal at home and in Indian offices, but they slow down an interviewer in New York, London, Toronto or Dubai. Swap them before the call:

Instead ofSay
“I passed out in 2024”“I graduated in 2024”
“I have a doubt”“I have a question”
“I will revert”“I will get back to you”
“Can we prepone it?”“Can we move it earlier?”
“Please do the needful”Say the actual request: “Could you send the offer details?”
“My CTC is 18 LPA”“My base salary is about … a year” (convert, and say base and bonus separately)
“I was out of station”“I was travelling”

Speak a little slower than you do at home and finish sentences clearly. Nobody expects you to change your accent; they need to follow your examples.
