Canada job interviews for Indians: Canadian experience, behavioural questions and references

For international students, new permanent residents and Indians interviewing with Canadian employers.

Updated 5 October 2026 · 7 min read · Published by Interview Sarthi, makers of Prep Sarthi (AI mock interviews) and Live Sarthi (interview help on Windows)

Short answer: Canadian interviews are behavioural and value soft skills: teamwork, communication and fit. When asked about “Canadian experience”, don't apologise: point to results that transfer, any Canadian work, volunteering or courses, and how you have already adapted to the local workplace. Give salary as an annual range in Canadian dollars.

Behavioural questions come first

Expect “Tell me about a time you…” questions on teamwork, conflict, customer service and handling mistakes. Canadian interviewers listen for how you work with people as much as for technical skill. Answer with STAR (situation, task, action, result) and use “I” for your part; the STAR guide has worked examples.

The “Canadian experience” question

Newcomers hear some version of “Do you have Canadian experience?” often. In Ontario, the Human Rights Commission's policy says a strict requirement for Canadian experience is discrimination on its face, unless the employer can justify it. You don't need to argue that in the interview; answer the worry behind the question, which is usually “will you fit how we work here?”

Script

“My full-time experience is from India, five years in accounts payable for a company with clients in North America, so I already work with Canadian and US suppliers and month-end deadlines. Since moving I've finished the CPA bridging course and volunteer as a treasurer for a community organisation in Brampton. I'd bring the same accuracy and speed here from the first week.”

Transferable results, any local proof (a course, volunteering, contract work), and a confident close. Replace the details with your own.

References are actually checked

Canadian employers often call two or three references before an offer. Choose people who can describe your work, including managers in India, tell them to expect a call at Canadian hours, and give the employer their email as well as a phone number with the country code.

Work permits and sponsorship

Many post-graduation work permits are open, which means no employer sponsorship is needed while the permit is valid. Say so in one sentence; scripts are in the sponsorship guide.

Salary: annual, in Canadian dollars

  • The Government of Canada's Job Bank publishes wages by occupation and region, a good free starting point.
  • Some provinces, including British Columbia, require employers to put a pay range in job postings. Use it to anchor your answer.
  • Answer as a range: “Based on the posting and Job Bank data for Toronto, I'm looking at 70 to 78 thousand a year.” (Example figures; use your own research.)

Indian-English phrases that confuse interviewers abroad

These are normal at home and in Indian offices, but they slow down an interviewer in New York, London, Toronto or Dubai. Swap them before the call:

Instead ofSay
“I passed out in 2024”“I graduated in 2024”
“I have a doubt”“I have a question”
“I will revert”“I will get back to you”
“Can we prepone it?”“Can we move it earlier?”
“Please do the needful”Say the actual request: “Could you send the offer details?”
“My CTC is 18 LPA”“My base salary is about … a year” (convert, and say base and bonus separately)
“I was out of station”“I was travelling”

Speak a little slower than you do at home and finish sentences clearly. Nobody expects you to change your accent; they need to follow your examples.
