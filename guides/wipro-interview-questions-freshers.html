Check the official hiring process first

Elite and Turbo: read the current terms

Program names alone do not tell you your salary, interview difficulty or contract terms. Check the current vacancy and offer, and ask the recruiter about anything unclear before accepting.

Technical questions to practise

  • “Explain your project, and what was your exact role?” Be clear about the second half. Have one honest sentence ready: which module you built, which you didn't. Claiming the whole group project is the fastest credibility loss available.
  • One language, properly. Java: OOP pillars, exception handling, ArrayList vs array. Python: lists vs tuples, loops vs comprehensions, file handling. C: pointers, malloc, string functions. Declare the language you actually know. The questions follow your claim.
  • SQL practice. A join written live, DELETE vs TRUNCATE, what an index does. One smooth query answers three doubts about you at once.
  • Definition ladders: OS → process vs thread → what a deadlock is; networking → what happens when you type a URL. Breadth checks: a clean two-sentence answer beats a lecture.
  • For Turbo: live problem-solving, arrays, strings, recursion, with your thinking narrated. Practising out loud matters more than practising more problems.

HR answers to practise for Wipro

  • Why this role? Choose one specific responsibility from the current posting and connect it to something you have done or want to learn.
  • When can you join, and can you relocate? Give your actual availability and constraints. Correct any old information in your application.
  • What are the offer terms? Read the current pay, training, location and any agreement before accepting. Ask questions if anything is unclear.

Mistakes to avoid during practice

  • Skipping an assigned writing task. Practise writing if it appears in your assessment invitation.
  • A resume claim that dies on the first follow-up: a language you listed but can't code in, a project role you didn't have.
  • Contradicting the application form on relocation, backlogs or dates.
  • Agreeing to terms you have not understood. Ask the recruiter to clarify the actual offer.
  • Panic silence. Ask for ten seconds, structure, answer. The pause is trainable.

Use the language requested in your invitation. If you want to switch languages, ask the interviewer first. Our Hinglish answer examples can help you practise when that language is permitted.
