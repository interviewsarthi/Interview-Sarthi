Interview help in Hinglish, because your interviews aren't in one language

Prepare for interviews that mix Hindi and English. Practise preserving technical words, explaining your own projects and matching the language agreed for the interview. Live Sarthi, our Windows app, suggests answers in English or Hinglish during the call, following the interviewer's language (30 minutes free, then unlimited use from ₹99 for 2 days); Prep Sarthi lets you practise out loud first, free for 7 minutes.

Why mixed-language questions need practice

A question can start in Hindi and end with an English technical term. Test whether a transcript preserves the meaning of both parts, and practise answering in the language your interviewer expects.

What answering in Hinglish looks like

Interviewer asks:

"Aapne Redis kyun use kiya us project mein?"

Illustrative practice answer (not a recorded product result):

"Honestly, latency ki wajah se. Session lookups har request pe ho rahe the, Postgres pe ~40 ms lag raha tha. Redis pe 1 ms ke andar, aur TTL se expiry free mein mil gayi."

Notice what that answer is not: it's not a translated paragraph, and it's not textbook Hindi. It's the code-mixed way engineers in India actually talk, technical words in English, connective tissue in Hindi, grounded in the Redis project from your CV, because generic essays are the fastest way to sound like you're reading.

When the interview is in English, so are the answers

The tool mirrors the interviewer. Formal English question → crisp English answer. Hindi/Hinglish question → Hinglish answer. You don't configure anything; it follows the conversation the way you would.

Who this helps most

Candidates strongest in Hindi who freeze composing English answers under pressure. The draft gives you the sentence structure, you deliver it your way. Engineers interviewing at Indian services and product companies, where HR rounds especially run bilingual. Anyone re-entering interviews after years. The vocabulary comes back when it's on screen in the mix of languages you think in.

A five-minute Hinglish practice checklist

  1. Choose one real project. Note your role, the problem and what changed.
  2. Explain it once in English and once in Hinglish. Keep names, dates and numbers consistent.
  3. Ask a friend to change language halfway through a follow-up.
  4. If using a transcript or AI draft, mark missing technical terms and invented personal claims.
  5. Repeat the answer without reading the draft. Keep only improvements you can explain yourself.

Start with a Hinglish self-introduction, continue with HR answer examples, then learn how to structure mixed-language answers. The checklist is a practice exercise, not a performance benchmark.
