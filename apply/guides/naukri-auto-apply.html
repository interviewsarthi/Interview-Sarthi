How to start auto apply on Naukri

  1. Get Apply Sarthi for Windows. It is being published on the Microsoft Store. Until the Store link is on the Apply Sarthi page, leave your email there and the link is sent to you.
  2. Add your CV once and say which jobs you want.
  3. Sign in to Naukri yourself, the ordinary way, inside the app’s own browser window. Apply Sarthi never stores your job-site passwords.

Why most tools cannot reach Naukri

When we wrote the first version of this guide in September 2026, Naukri refused two things that nearly every automation product depends on: plain requests from a server and browsers with no visible window. A tool that runs on a vendor’s server and fetches pages the ordinary way gets nothing usable back.

What works is a real browser window on your own computer, used the way a person uses one. That is slower and cannot be run cheaply at scale on a server, which is why products marketed on volume leave Naukri out.

When a product advertises “30+ job boards” without listing them, assume Naukri is not among them until the company names it.

What is actually possible

Three different things get called auto apply on Naukri, and only two of them exist.

  • Finding open jobs that fit you. Workable. In our snapshot of 18 September 2026 there were 6,293 open Naukri postings to match against a CV.
  • Filling and sending the application. Workable, from a real browser window on your own computer, using a Naukri session you signed in to yourself. This is how the Apply Sarthi app applies on Naukri.
  • Bulk submission from a server. Not workable, and not something to buy from anyone claiming otherwise.

Naukri’s recommended jobs are not the same as matching

Naukri suggests roles based on the profile you filled in on Naukri — your stated title, your stated skills, your stated experience. That is a good keyword match against what you typed, which is not the same as a match against what your CV actually says.

The gap shows up most for people changing function or returning after a break, where the profile fields describe the last job rather than the next one. Matching against the CV text itself, as a whole document, tends to surface roles the profile-based suggestions miss.

The daily number worth keeping to

Sites that need a login are the ones with something to lose. An account that sends a very large number of applications in a day can be limited by the site, and a restriction lands on the account you need during the search itself.

The practical split: keep the count modest on boards where you hold an account, and put volume into company career pages, which have no account attached.

Good habits when applying on Naukri

  1. Keep your Naukri profile current anyway — recruiters search it directly, independently of your applications.
  2. Use a tool that shows you what it answered. Apply Sarthi lists the answers it gave on every application, and its “Fill only” setting leaves the submit button to you.
  3. Tailor the CV per role. A Naukri application carries whichever CV you attach, and the attached file is what a recruiter opens.
  4. Check the first three applications by hand before trusting the fourth.

Will Naukri restrict my account for using an auto-apply tool?

A job site can limit an account that behaves unlike a person, and very high daily volume is the behaviour most likely to attract that. A tool that works in a real browser window on your own computer, signed in as you, at a modest daily pace, is much closer to you applying yourself, but no tool can promise an account will never be limited. Keep your daily numbers modest on any site where you hold an account; Apply Sarthi keeps a safe daily pace on Naukri for that reason, never solves “are you human” checks, and has a “Fill only” setting that leaves Submit to you.

Is Naukri or LinkedIn better for applying in India?

Both, for different reasons. In our snapshot of 18 September 2026 Naukri held 6,293 open postings and LinkedIn 8,110, but the overlap is small and the employer mix differs. Company career pages outweigh either individually.
